# Published-plan comparison

Primary sources accessed 25 September 2026. This is a comparison of authored training approaches, not clinical validation, proof of individual race readiness, or a claim that one coach's plan defines the only correct workload. No complete external schedule is reproduced. Mile distances below use 1 mile = 1.609344 km.

That is a different one-workout design. A label such as “400 m intervals” alone does not identify the intended effort: Stride must retain separate interval/repetition roles and use current fitness. |
| Intermediate 10K | [Higdon Intermediate 10K](https://www.halhigdon.com/training-programs/10k-training/intermediate-10k/) targets experienced runners averaging 15–25 weekly miles. The tempo session's total duration includes easy opening/closing running. | The same Stride 32/10 km, five-day, one-workout sample peaks at 41.8/13 km, with 16 maximum weekly quality minutes. Compare hard work separately from whole-session time; counting every warm-up/recovery minute as tempo would overstate the dose. |

The differing peak is not by itself a software defect: the supplied baseline, target bands, selected frequency and explicit capacity must agree with the resulting forecast. |

## Cross-checks against other primary plans

[B.A.A. 10K Level One](https://www.baa.org/races/boston-10k/info-for-athletes/b-a-a-10k-training/) includes tempo in its opening preparation phase despite its novice label. [B.A.A. marathon Level One](https://www.baa.org/races/boston-marathon/info-for-athletes/boston-marathon-training/) starts around 25 weekly miles, grows toward 40, and uses long runs of 16–18 miles with quality sessions. These establish that “beginner” names can refer to substantially different starting populations. They do not justify adding speedwork to Stride's zero-history NHS course or easy-only first-race branch. The B.A.A. tables have not been copied into code.

## Review conclusions

- Preserve stated opening mileage and longest-run capacity; reject an impossible starting allocation rather than silently rewriting it. Longer calendars do not create completed preparation.
- Treat entry numbers, 10% growth, four-week recovery spacing, taper percentages and readiness screens as Stride's authored policy. The referenced plans do not establish universal safe limits for these values.
- Fitness zones belong to the current benchmark; the event changes workout selection and race-rhythm prescriptions. A target finish time alone should not accelerate easy or threshold training.
- Preserve the endpoint: a fixed distance with a slower target requires more planning time; a fixed timed run retains its time and changes estimated distance. Walking and recovery remain explicit.
- Slow-runner constraints require truthful outcomes. At 15 min/km, a 28 km preparation run needs 420 minutes; a 300-minute cap cannot support that forecast. A limitation is preferable to an invented faster pace.
- Confirmed during this review: retained manual pace settings fabricated distance ranges for untargeted foundation lessons. Generation and restoration now clear that pace evidence for the lesson estimate; logs of actual measured distance remain separate.

The executable audit and its independent outcome oracle are in `scripts/verify-foundation-pace-stress.mjs`. Initial and corrected runs are retained alongside this report. The intermediate values above are descriptive examples, not newly asserted biological thresholds or copied reference schedules.
