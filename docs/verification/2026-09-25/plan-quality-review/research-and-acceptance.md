Primary sources checked 25 September 2026. These are comparisons of published coaching choices, not universal physiological limits. Stride's proposed numerical bounds below are explicit product policy. No complete source schedule is reproduced.

## Resolve the opening-input conflict explicitly

For four easy running days, weekly distance `W`, declared long run `L`, and a product maximum easy/long ratio `r=0.8`, total capacity is:

`maximum W = L + 3 × r × L`.

Consequently, **30 km/week, an 8 km long run, four days and zero workouts cannot retain both baselines and enforce 80%**. The maximum is 27.2 km. Preserving 30 km would require a long run of at least 8.824 km; rounding that to 9 km adds 12.5% to the runner's reported longest run immediately.

The latest user request explicitly permits shortening easy runs or lengthening the long run. Prefer retaining the familiar opening long run and showing the lower coherent weekly prescription, with its difference from the declared recent mileage. Do not overwrite the reported history or claim the actual first week still equals 30 km. The 80%-only illustration would require disclosure of 27.2 km and a 2.8 km reduction; the implementation's additional 65% short-support rule yields 26 km for the reviewed Tuesday/Wednesday/Friday/Sunday example. A longer first long run needs available time, a defensible bounded progression and clear explanation; it must not be created just to repair a label.

Quality days change the arithmetic. Their whole-session distance contains warm-up, running repetitions and recovery; they must not receive unlimited easy padding just to meet an arbitrary weekly total. Nor should a missing quality session be hidden by relabeling an easy run.

## Proposed independent acceptance checks

1. **Visible hierarchy.** In ordinary and recovery weeks of standard named-distance plans, ordinary easy outings should not exceed 80% of the explicit long run, allowing metre-level arithmetic tolerance. Report mixed-taper weeks separately because actual-day taper factors may differ. A marathon may contain one explicitly named midweek endurance/medium-long outing at up to 90% of its long run; this is a deliberate product exception, not a universal coaching threshold. A road-plan endurance label remains subject to 80%. Race-week and zero-history lessons require their own role rules. The allocator also keeps short support near the long run or consecutive running days at 65%; this can lower the 27.2 km theoretical opening capacity illustrated above further.
2. **Truthful input adaptation.** Report declared and prescribed opening weekly/long distances, the difference, and why it occurred. Preserve input/history as recorded. Reject or mark genuinely inadequate preparation rather than silently increasing long-run time, pace, frequency or a hard-work dose.
3. **Whole-run progression.** Newly allocated long-run targets should use understandable whole-kilometre steps after the opening anchor, with reduced weeks clearly identified. Supporting load should grow with the programme's capacity and then reduce coherently in recovery and taper. Edited fixed-time estimates are not newly allocated distance targets.
4. **Useful variety.** Compare actual main-set/recovery signatures, ignoring titles, warm-up padding and numeric pace-label refreshes. A progression from shorter to longer repetitions or a controlled switch between continuous and divided work is real change. Merely renaming six identical one-minute repetitions is not.
5. **Bounded repetition.** For an unconstrained standard `varied` programme with quality work, flag more than two consecutive ordinary weeks containing the same quality main sets and recoveries. Familiar-mode and genuinely constrained early exposures may repeat deliberately. A repeated recipe after a reduced week is not automatically a defect, and some repeated benchmark sessions are useful.
6. **No intensity manufactured for entertainment.** Zero-workout plans remain easy; first-race and learning programmes retain their own deliberate repetition. Variety must respect the selected workout count, recent quality history, complete repetitions, recovery spacing and the available time.
7. **Regression integrity.** Completed and delivery-protected sessions remain byte-for-byte stable under future variety changes. Canonical steps, estimated totals, JSON restoration and quality accounting remain consistent. Generation runtime is measured separately from training quality; fast valid code can still produce a poor plan.
