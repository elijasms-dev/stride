# 5K and 10K training research — 23 September 2026

Scope: published primary plans for new, developing, established and experienced runners. Reviewed 18 plan variants across five publishers: six Hal Higdon, six British Heart Foundation (BHF), three Boston Athletic Association (BAA), two Nike and one NHS. The findings below are paraphrased comparisons, not reproductions of their schedules. They inform an independently authored Stride policy; they are not evidence that one precise progression or percentage guarantees a safe or successful race.

## Conclusions supported by comparison

1. **Distance alone does not define the plan.** A new 5K runner may need run-walk training; an established 5K runner can reasonably maintain substantial weekly mileage and an easy run longer than 5 km. Labels such as beginner/intermediate are inconsistent between publishers. Existing volume, continuous-run ability, frequency and recent quality exposure are better inputs than self-description alone. [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/better-health/get-active/get-running-with-couch-to-5k/), [Higdon intermediate 5K](https://www.halhigdon.com/training-programs/5k-training/intermediate-5k/), [BHF beginner 10K](https://www.bhf.org.uk/-/media/files/how-you-can-help/events/events-rebranded-training-plans/10k-run-beginners.pdf).

2. **Zero, one and two quality sessions are all defensible choices.** Reviewed novice plans can be wholly easy; intermediate examples may use one; others use two. User-selected frequency should remain explicit, subject to transparent capacity constraints. Replacing an interval session with a few strides must not count as preserving it. [Higdon novice 10K](https://www.halhigdon.com/training-programs/10k-training/novice-10k/), [Higdon intermediate 10K](https://www.halhigdon.com/training-programs/10k-training/intermediate-10k/), [Nike 10K](https://www.nike.com/pdf/Nike-Run-Club-10K-Training-Plan-Audio-Guided-Runs.pdf).

3. **An easy long run supports the faster work; it is not automatically another hard workout.** Separate the user's weekday quality count from the long-run slot. For short races, prioritize a coherent interval/threshold progression, retaining mostly easy surrounding mileage. [Higdon advanced 5K](https://www.halhigdon.com/training-programs/5k-training/advanced-5k/), [Nike 5K](https://www.nike.com/pdf/Nike-Run-Club-5K-Training-Plan-Audio-Guided-Runs.pdf).

4. **Whole-session duration and hard-work duration are different quantities.** A published tempo-session label can include warm-up, gradual acceleration and cooldown. Do not convert every listed minute into threshold running, or fill an oversized weekly budget by padding a small quality set into an implausibly long workout. [Higdon advanced 10K](https://www.halhigdon.com/training-programs/10k-training/advanced-10k/).

5. **Holding, recovery and taper are part of progression.** Sources include plateaus, lighter weeks, test races and reduced pre-race load. No source comparison establishes a universal weekly percentage increase. Clean kilometre steps are a Stride product choice, with hold weeks to accommodate small baseline volumes. [Higdon novice 10K](https://www.halhigdon.com/training-programs/10k-training/novice-10k/), [BHF advanced 5K](https://www.bhf.org.uk/-/media/files/how-you-can-help/events/events-rebranded-training-plans/5k-run-advanced.pdf).

## Proposed Stride engineering policy — authored design, not copied prescriptions

- Scope new branches to exact named `5k`, `10k`, `half` goals. Keep marathon/custom/ultra behaviour outside this change.
- Start at the entered weekly volume and long run when the combination is feasible. If the baseline cannot fit the requested day/time/quality limits, return a precise conflict rather than increasing the volume, inventing runs, or silently changing the saved choice.
- Classify ability using established weekly volume, run frequency, recent long run and quality history. Pace selects workout targets, not the athlete's capacity tier. A fast recent race is not evidence of tolerance for extra running days or two hard sessions.
- Default developing completion runners to easy training. Preserve explicit 0/1/2 choices only when the entire schedule and useful work doses fit. Two total running days cannot simultaneously support two separated weekday workouts and a separate long run; reject that conflict clearly.
- Give a one-workout plan a coherent alternating development of threshold/aerobic strength and race-specific intervals. Give a two-workout plan complementary sessions rather than duplicate maximal work. Scale repetition count/work duration before raising pace; pace remains tied to current fitness.
- Use separate envelopes for main-set work and entire session. Suggested initial engine guardrails: approximately 6–12 hard minutes for an introductory quality session, larger doses only as existing capacity and progress support them; ordinary quality-session total time must not expand without bound to consume weekly mileage. These exact figures are engineering choices requiring tests, not direct source mandates.
- Bound the long-run target by timeline, starting long run, weekly budget and duration; permit established high-volume athletes to maintain a longer existing easy run. Do not force every 5K runner toward the same long-run ceiling. Build distance in integer-kilometre steps or hold; preserve an explicitly fractional starting anchor, then move to clean subsequent targets.
- Reserve easy support volume before adding another quality session. If an exact weekly baseline produces a large remainder, allocate it to appropriately sized easy runs rather than adding an hour before short repeats.
- Use a date-aware taper. Reduce training before the race, keep a small familiar stimulus where suitable, and exclude the race itself when checking whether training volume declines. Never shrink the main-set dose only to refill the session with easy padding.
- Validate actual step content and summed work minutes, not `hard: true` metadata. Every complete ordinary week must match the resolved weekday frequency. Exceptions must be explicit recovery/taper/partial-entry/adaptation weeks, not accidental gaps.
- Test low and high baselines, fractional anchors, new-but-running versus true-zero-history, 2–7 running days, all 0/1/2 selections, finish/improve, fast/slow benchmarks, short/long timelines, weekday races, time caps and preference edits. Retain marathon snapshots to prove preservation.

Published examples contain inconsistent terminology and occasional arithmetic/conversion labels. They were cross-checked for principles, not transcribed into Stride as authoritative templates. The implementation should preserve its own internally consistent distances, step arithmetic, pace units and validation contracts.
