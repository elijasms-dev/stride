# Source-based pacing contract

Reviewed 28 September 2026. This change governs pace instructions and their presentation. It does not certify Stride's existing adaptive schedules as exact copies of a published programme.

## Evidence before a target

A saved segment records its pace meaning, source and version, method, benchmark evidence where used, explanation and resolved target. Current performance, an intended race goal and a mathematical race equivalent are separate concepts. Existing completed, past, delivered and near-term protected workouts retain their saved prescriptions. Updating a benchmark uses the target review path rather than regenerating the schedule.

A direct numerical current-race target requires the matching race distance and the runner's confirmation that the result represents their present running. Date, course and result type remain descriptive. The app does not invent age decay, terrain correction or weekly pace improvement. Missing evidence retains the prescribed effort cue. A point pace is not expanded into an invented training range; device serialization must preserve that distinction.

## Primary instructions

- [NHS Couch to 5K](https://www.nhs.uk/better-health/get-active/get-running-with-couch-to-5k/couch-to-5k-running-plan/): comfortable effort and timed run/walk progression. Numerical targets and quality sessions are not added because a benchmark exists.
- [Higdon 5K Intermediate](https://www.halhigdon.com/training-programs/5k-training/intermediate-5k/): its 400m repetitions refer to mile/1500m pace; tempo is progressive effort rather than one constant threshold target.
- [Higdon 10K Intermediate](https://www.halhigdon.com/training-programs/10k-training/intermediate-10k/): its 400m repetitions refer to 5K pace. A supplied 25:00 5K gives 300 seconds/km and 120 seconds per 400m through direct arithmetic, without introducing a conversion coefficient.
- [Higdon Half Intermediate 2](https://www.halhigdon.com/training-programs/half-marathon-training/intermediate-2-half-marathon/): the schedule's repetitions refer to 5K pace, while separate pace runs refer to intended half-marathon pace. Prose offers 10K as an alternative to the table's 5K prescription; the table is the explicit default, not an averaged target.
- [Hansons official beginner marathon PDF](https://s3.us-central-1.wasabisys.com/hansons/Beginner_Marathon_-_new.pdf): tempo denotes goal marathon pace; strength uses that pace minus 10 seconds per mile. Any conversion must preserve the original units and named source.
- [V.O2 2019 update](https://support.vdoto2.com/2019/08/updated-vdot-paces-39-for-greater-accuracy/): training paces below VDOT 39 changed independently of the race-derived score. A match to an older formula does not establish current calculator compatibility. No unpublished correction coefficients are reconstructed.
- [Vickers and Vertosick, 2016](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13102-016-0052-y): recreational marathon predictions from shorter performances have substantial limitations. Race equivalence is not sufficient evidence of sustainable marathon pace.

These are source-specific instructions, not interchangeable intensity systems. Existing untagged Stride recipes must be identified honestly; a generic label or selected event distance does not authorize assigning a Higdon source.

## UI and manual choices

The pace editor presents the runner's reference and only the roles found in the plan. Automatic values are never copied wholesale into manual preferences. Personal overrides remain independent and labelled as runner-supplied. Effort-only beginner, recovery and skill segments retain their qualitative guidance.

Drawn graphics are presentation, not invented data. Range endpoints, chart values and chronology remain exact. Session sequences are explicitly schematic, with readable source instructions and accurate repetitions. Planned distance and recorded distance are separate; unknown records are not zero.
