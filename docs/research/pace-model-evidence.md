# Stride pace model: primary-source research

Research and implementation record, 2026-09-25. Recommendations below distinguish source findings from product-design inferences.

## Decision supported by the evidence

Use a current-fitness estimate to derive effort paces; let the selected 5K/10K/half/marathon plan determine race-specific targets, workout composition, duration, and progression. Holding benchmark and fitness constant, merely changing the selected plan should not change easy/threshold/VO2 interval effort zones. This invariance is an architectural inference from the coaching definitions below, not a separately validated clinical rule. Marathon readiness needs additional treatment; a short-race equivalence alone is not a reliable marathon goal.

## Source findings and exact scope

1. **Current fitness, not aspirational goal time.** [Official V.O2 FAQ](https://support.vdoto2.com/v-o2-faq/) says VDOT represents current running fitness; a recent representative race/time trial is preferred. With no race, it permits a current estimated race result, then typical conversational easy pace as a weaker fallback. Easy should remain conversational and quality workouts hard but achievable. It distinguishes VDOT from measured VO2max and notes that athletes have different strengths at different distances. Implication: never silently turn the desired finish time into the baseline fitness estimate. These are the method owner's coaching instructions, not independent proof of every formula or multiplier.

2. **What training labels mean.** [Jack Daniels, official V.O2 training definitions](https://vdoto2.com/learn-more/training-definitions): easy is conversational and includes recovery, warm-up and ordinary long runs; terrain/weather affect appropriate speed. Threshold approximates an intensity raceable for nearly one hour in ideal conditions. VO2 interval work is hard, not above VO2max workload, with 1–5 minute workbouts; associated race duration is about 10–12 minutes. Repetitions are short, around current mile/1500m intensity, with full recovery. Marathon work is anticipated sustainable marathon intensity and should wait until the runner is sufficiently prepared. Implication: distance labels alone do not define identical physiological stimuli across abilities; long runs need not be a separately faster physiological zone. Do not interpret the page's subjective effort percentages as percentages of running speed or pace.

3. **Equivalence is not a finish-time guarantee.** [Official V.O2 Pace Calculator explanation](https://news.vdoto2.com/2023/04/pace-calculator/) explicitly characterizes equivalent performances as physiological equivalents rather than predictions. Individual strengths, training volume, and training emphasis affect realization. It recommends a benchmark lasting at least 10–12 minutes when deriving threshold pace. Therefore display “equivalent performance” separately from “goal” or a readiness-adjusted race estimate.

4. **Riegel and the marathon.** [Vickers & Vertosick 2016, original study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13102-016-0052-y), [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27570626/): survey N=2,303 recreational runners; training/validation split 2:1. Riegel was well calibrated up to half-marathon in this sample, but marathon estimates were at least 10 minutes too fast for about half the runners. Main comparison used exponent k=1.07 and the longest available shorter race. Sensitivity checks at 1.06 and 1.08 did not reverse the finding. Their improved marathon models included prior race performance and typical weekly mileage. They excluded very difficult/very fast races and adjusted intermediate difficulty categories. Do not describe the result as proof that all runners need one replacement exponent or an arbitrary percentage penalty.

5. **Benchmark recency and conditions.** [Official V.O2, Tracking Your VDOT](https://news.vdoto2.com/2022/06/tracking-your-vdot/) recommends current performance and reassessing training/fitness if the score has not changed for six weeks. It favors roughly 2-mile through 10K tests, partly to reduce course/weather distortion, and discusses fitness loss after time off. This supports storing benchmark date, effort/context, and subsequent training interruption. It does **not** validate a universal 6-week expiration or a fixed per-week decay. Flag lower confidence and request reassessment; any age thresholds/decay coefficients are explicit product assumptions requiring validation.

6. **Critical speed is useful, but not interchangeable with every threshold.** [Nixon et al. 2021, original experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34351531/) tested 10 well-trained male runners: CS and maximal lactate steady-state speeds differed significantly (16.4 vs. 15.2 km/h averages). Oxygen uptake stayed stable just below CS and rose to maximum just above it. This supports a physiological basis for CS while warning against calling it identical to lactate threshold or assuming one universal percentage maps it to every training zone. Generalization is limited by the small, trained, male sample.

7. **CS needs suitable observations.** [Hunter et al., Remote Determination of CS/CP, original study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37888148/), [institutional full text](https://repository.londonmet.ac.uk/8870/1/IJSPP-2023-0276_online.pdf): 23 recreational runners (4 female); best 3-, 7-, and 12-minute training segments over six weeks were compared with dedicated trials and 3-minute all-out tests. CS/CP showed agreement across approaches, whereas D′/W′ agreement was limited. A CS fit from several appropriate efforts is a future option; a single arbitrary benchmark does not identify both CS and D′.

8. **Marathon durability differs among runners.** [Smyth et al. 2022, original study of 82,303 runners](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-022-01680-5) found substantial individual variation in heart-rate/speed decoupling during marathons. Including decoupling traits improved their performance-model error from 6.45% to 5.16%. This supports separating aerobic fitness from marathon durability. Their group averages and observed marathon decoupling are not ready-made prospective pace prescriptions for an individual beginner.

9. **No-benchmark beginners can use effort and time.** [Official NHS Couch to 5K](https://www.nhs.uk/better-health/get-active/get-running-with-couch-to-5k/) begins with one-minute runs and 90-second walks at a self-appropriate pace, builds toward 30 minutes of continuous running, and permits a slower program progression. A beginner fallback therefore need not invent min/km precision. Prefer comfortable run/walk and time targets, then calibrate when a representative observation exists.

## Proposed implementation contract (design inference)

- Keep `benchmark`, `currentFitness`, `trainingEffortPaces`, `equivalentRacePaces`, and `goalRacePace` separate. Store model/version, source input, benchmark date, confidence reasons, and assumptions with results.
- Prefer a current representative measured performance. Preserve source quality (race/time trial, estimated, conversational easy pace); do not silently treat these as equally reliable. Hills, extreme weather, nonmaximal effort, uncertain distance, old results, or time off lower confidence. Avoid unsupported “corrections” that make a difficult-course result faster.
- For one valid benchmark, a documented coaching model can provide estimated effort ranges. Riegel is primarily an equivalence model, not a validation for fixed easy/threshold/interval multipliers of the chosen goal distance's pace. A VDOT implementation would need separately verified formulas/tables and reference outputs; this research does not certify numerical coefficients.
- For marathon predictions, either use a separately validated readiness model with its required inputs or clearly present an equivalence with limited confidence. Do not invent a universal 1.10–1.15 exponent or label a hand-tuned multiplier research validated.
- With no reliable benchmark, show effort descriptions and duration/run-walk targets; numeric pace can be unavailable or explicitly provisional. A future benchmark can recalibrate all plans consistently.
- Test plan-invariance of easy/threshold/interval outputs for the same fitness, goal-time independence, monotonic response to faster representative benchmarks, units/pace inversion, positive finite ordered ranges, invalid/missing inputs, stale/poor-quality provenance, and explicit marathon uncertainty. Numeric oracle tests must use the chosen model's official reference outputs.

No primary source retrieved here establishes a single mathematically exact algorithm suitable for every runner, benchmark quality, race distance, and workout. These sources support the architecture and caveats, not arbitrary constants.

## Implemented model: stride-daniels-1

`lib/fitness-model.ts` computes a continuous Daniels/Gilbert performance index. With speed `v` in metres/minute and duration `t` in minutes:

```
oxygen(v) = -4.6 + 0.182258*v + 0.000104*v*v
fraction(t) = 0.8 + 0.1894393*exp(-0.012778*t) + 0.2989558*exp(-0.1932605*t)
VDOT = oxygen(v) / fraction(t)
```

The exact coefficients are printed in [Fan-Yun Lan's original research dissertation, Harvard 2021, methods p.19 / PDF p.37](https://dash.harvard.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/97701053-1a3b-48f9-b055-e3ad4045fe22/content#page=37). This verifies the published computation, not clinical validation of Stride's implementation. Inversion of the quadratic yields speed at each oxygen-demand fraction; percentages are never multiplied directly by pace.

Bands follow [Daniels, *Daniels' Running Formula*, fourth edition](https://us.humankinetics.com/products/daniels-running-formula-4th-edition), Figure 4.1: easy 59–74%, M intensity 75–84%, threshold 85–88%, interval 95–100%. Figure and novice Table 5.3 were visually verified in the [school-hosted author text](https://mshsxc.com/DanielsRunningFormula.pdf#page=83). Stride calls M intensity **steady**, retaining `tempo` as its legacy alias; this is a product vocabulary choice, not a marathon-performance prediction. Threshold intensity varies with training status; the book discusses lower fractions for less-trained runners. Repetition uses current equivalent 1500m-to-mile speeds with full recovery, as described in the official coaching definitions above, rather than an arbitrary supra-maximal multiplier. No training target is calculated from the goal event's Riegel pace.

The numeric compatibility API uses representative oxygen fractions: easy .70, steady .795, threshold .88, interval .975. The latter two midpoint choices (.795/.975) are implementation choices within published bands; they do not reproduce every official calculator value. The new target API uses the full bands. Printed benchmark tables and continuous formulas differ slightly, especially for novices.

Independent numerical checks come from [Jack Daniels' authored 2000 article and linked Tables 1/2](https://www.coacheseducation.com/endur/jack-daniels-nov-00.php), not values calculated by this implementation. Five VDOT levels (30/40/50/60/70), their 5K times and E/T/I/R paces are tested; score tolerance is .08 and pace tolerance 3 seconds/km, covering printed rounding, the chosen interval representative, and the current-mile repetition approximation. Additional checks use the printed VDOT50 10K and mile equivalents. The fourth-edition novice table supplies 5K42:24, VDOT20 and threshold8:41/km as a lower-domain check.

The actionable range API supports VDOT20–85 and road-distance benchmarks through marathon. These bounds are product support limits based on the verified novice/full tables, not physiological validity thresholds. Outside them, the benchmark is retained and `deriveFitness` returns null paces/ranges plus a reason; consumers retain effort guidance. `calculateTrainingPaces` preserves its old numeric API, including a separately derived Riegel `marathon` field, for compatibility. Its extrapolated values must not bypass the range API's support check. The old `fitnessPaceRange` ±5-second helper is reserved for a single race-equivalent execution tolerance; it is never a confidence interval or training zone.

Metadata and dates influence warnings only. No time decay, terrain adjustment or inferred fitness improvement is applied. Explicit manual easy ranges take precedence in scheduling; otherwise the automatic easy band is funded at its slow edge while respecting a slower declared easy pace. `pacingEvidence` explains conflicting inputs. With no supported benchmark, the existing scheduling estimate remains a duration-budget fallback, not a prescribed physiological pace.
