# Beginner distance plans: evidence and Stride adaptation

Primary sources checked 24 September 2026. These references inform the beginner
plan design; a generated schedule and software validation do not establish an
individual runner's race readiness.

## Plan identity and starting capacity

`profile.planLevel: 'beginner'` is an explicit choice for completion-focused 5K,
10K, half-marathon or marathon preparation. `standard` retains the established
training path. Event experience and current running capacity are separate: an
established runner attempting a first marathon is not a zero-history runner.

For all four distances, a genuine zero-running baseline starts with the timed
NHS foundation. The eventual event remains the goal, while the current programme
remains foundation. Below-base runners need a bridge; extra calendar weeks do not
constitute evidence that they have completed it.

## Foundation and bridge

[NHS Couch to 5K](https://www.nhs.uk/better-health/get-active/get-running-with-couch-to-5k/couch-to-5k-running-plan/)
contains 27 lessons across nine weeks, with three running days and rest between
them. It starts with short run/walk intervals and ends with three 30-minute
continuous runs. Walking warm-up and cooldown are additional to running time.
There is no prescribed distance, speed session or race taper. Thirty minutes
completed does not mean 5 km measured.

Stride keeps those lessons timed and records comfortable completion. Its stage
reviews, fatigue checks, minimum stage duration and evidence windows are authored
product rules, not NHS certification criteria. Graduation triggers a capacity
review rather than an automatic distance-plan handoff. Measured distance,
execution, routine and recovery must support the next block; unknown distance
or unknown continuous-running execution remain unknown.

For context, Higdon's separate
[novice base plan](https://www.halhigdon.com/training-programs/base-training/novice-base-training/)
opens at 9 running miles weekly and a 3-mile longest run. Its
[30-week Novice Supreme](https://www.halhigdon.com/training-programs/marathon-training/novice-supreme/)
combines 12 base weeks with 18 marathon weeks. Even that longer programme has an
existing-capacity assumption. Nine foundation weeks plus a published race block
is arithmetic only; bridging, repeats and recovery can require longer.

## Alternative populations and methods

- [Bupa's beginner 10K](https://www.bupa.co.uk/newsroom/ourviews/running-training-plans)
  explicitly assumes run/walk 5 km in under 40 minutes. Its
  [eight-week PDF](https://www.bupa.co.uk/~/media/Images/HealthManagement/Infographics/running-plans/10k-beginner.pdf)
  uses three runs, mixed time/distance prescriptions, later intervals and a
  reduced long run before race week. The prerequisite is specific to that plan;
  it is not a universal speed test for entering Stride.
- [London Marathon's 2026 beginner plan](https://www.londonmarathonevents.co.uk/sites/default/files/2025-10/2026_TCSLM_Beginner_Training_Plan.pdf)
  uses 16 weeks and three weekly outings with walking, running and later brief
  faster efforts. The first week totals 120 elapsed minutes but only 50 running
  minutes. The longest timed outing reaches 210 minutes before the final
  reductions. Later distance alternatives are choices, not promised equivalents
  or a universal three-and-a-half-hour limit.
- [runDisney/Galloway half-marathon](https://cdn3.parksmedia.wdprapps.disney.com/media/rd/redesign/running-training-programs/HalfMarathon_Training_Program.pdf)
  assumes a gentle 2-mile walk/run within two weeks. Two 30-minute maintenance
  sessions support alternating longer/shorter weekends; race is week 18 and
  week 19 is recovery. Its 14-mile peak and planned walking belong to that method.
  The separate
  [marathon programme](https://cdn4.parksmedia.wdprapps.disney.com/media/rd/redesign/running-training-programs/Marathon_Training_Program.pdf)
  has 29 weeks, two 30-minute weekday outings and weekend endurance reaching
  26 miles before reduced weeks. These are distinct run/walk strategies, not
  interchangeable ladders for a continuous-running plan.
- Beginner labels do not imply easy-only or zero-history training.
  [B.A.A. 10K Level One](https://www.baa.org/races/boston-10k/info-for-athletes/b-a-a-10k-training/)
  already contains tempo in its opening week;
  [B.A.A. half Level One](https://www.baa.org/races/boston-half/info-for-athletes/boston-half-training/)
  starts with an 11–14-mile week;
  [B.A.A. marathon Level One](https://www.baa.org/races/boston-marathon/info-for-athletes/boston-marathon-training/)
  assumes roughly 25 weekly miles and adds quality. These are comparisons, not
  copied Stride schedules. The B.A.A. pages reserve reproduction rights.

## Stride's adaptation contract

The rules below are independently authored implementation policy. They must not
be presented as quotations, clinical thresholds or guaranteed finish criteria.

The initial `first-race-v1` screen uses these rounded product settings:

| Event    | Minimum weekly / recent long distance | Selected running days | Model length | Taper before event | Long-run target                         |
| -------- | ------------------------------------- | --------------------- | ------------ | ------------------ | --------------------------------------- |
| 5K       | 7.5 / 2.5 km                          | 3                     | 8 weeks      | 7 days             | 5 km                                    |
| 10K      | 12 / 5 km                             | 3                     | 8 weeks      | 7 days             | 9 km                                    |
| Half     | 18 / 6 km                             | 3–4                   | 12 weeks     | 14 days            | 16 km                                   |
| Marathon | 24 / 10 km                            | 4                     | 18 weeks     | 21 days            | 32 km; preparation screen minimum 28 km |

All require at least three current running days. These are Stride eligibility
and forecasting choices; for example, the half's two-week taper differs from
the source's final-week reduction. Supporting-volume and repeated-exposure
checks are additional product screens. A target is subject to capacity and
feedback, not promised mileage or a guarantee of completion.

1. **Eligibility follows the opening demand.** Consider current frequency,
   weekly distance, recent longest outing and available time together. Never
   raise the baseline to make a selected plan eligible, or infer it from a goal
   time. A first race alone does not require a reduced baseline.
2. **Preserve the real start.** Retain the declared first complete week's total
   and longest outing, including fractional distances. A partial calendar week
   truncates that week's schedule; it does not redefine capacity. A lower base
   needs a bridge and an explicit preparation-gap review.
3. **Completion plans develop easy support.** Default beginner race blocks use
   easy/conversational support and endurance. Choosing a harder workout count
   requires an explicit compatible choice; never silently add or erase it.
   Three-run 5K/10K and three-or-four-run half benchmarks differ from the
   conventional four-run marathon benchmark. Do not squeeze a four-run
   schedule into three days while retaining its preparation claim.
4. **Fund progression and recovery together.** Long-run increases must fit the
   weekly total, supporting runs, real availability and duration limits. Hold
   the last build anchor across cutbacks. Additional cutback frequency,
   progression caps, rounded entry bands and peak targets are Stride policies.
   Keep their provenance separate from source facts.
5. **Taper against the real event date.** Short-event race-week reductions,
   half-marathon finish preparation and the conventional marathon's three-week
   taper are distinct. Extra time supports preparation; a short calendar does
   not justify compressing lessons or catching up during taper.
6. **Keep time, distance and execution truthful.** Foundation stays timed.
   Source-informed distance work still has to fit time limits at the applicable
   planning pace. A 32 km outing at 8 min/km needs 256 minutes, not 210.
   Planned walking must remain explicit and cannot prove continuous running.
   A separate run/walk method needs walking-capacity and elapsed-time evidence.
7. **Check the emitted plan.** Assess actual running/walking duration, distance,
   frequency, easy support, endurance exposures, peak timing and reduced weeks.
   Date changes, missed runs, fatigue, preference edits and recovery imports
   must preserve plan level and completed evidence. A valid data structure is
   not evidence of sufficient race preparation.
